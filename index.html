Chapter Five
The Muscle Dispute: A Forensic Investigation Into a Flex of Unknown Authenticity

Of all the items in this dossier, the muscle situation is the one that has occupied our forensic anthropology department the longest. The original brief states, with the brevity that marks a truly confident claim, that the subject "flexed fake muscles." This sentence contains a subject, a verb, and a qualifier, and it raises, upon reflection, an extraordinary number of questions that our team has been working through methodically and without, as of press time, reaching a satisfactory resolution.

The first question is definitional. What constitutes a fake muscle, in the context of a flex? There are several interpretive frameworks available. Under the first framework, "fake" refers to the absence of muscle mass: the subject performed the physical gesture of a flex without the corresponding anatomical substance to back it up. The arm went into the classic position. The bicep did not substantially respond. The performance exceeded the reality. This is the most literal interpretation and produces an image that is both vivid and forensically plausible.

Under the second framework, "fake" is used loosely, as an intensifier or descriptor, to convey that the flex was theatrical, exaggerated, or performed for comic rather than demonstrative effect. In this reading, there may be perfectly adequate muscles present. The "fake" refers not to their absence but to the self-aware, performative character of the flex itself. The subject knew the flex was for effect. The audience knew the flex was for effect. Everyone was in on it. The muscles, real or otherwise, were beside the point.

"She flexed. The muscles are disputed. The flex itself is not in dispute. The flex happened. We can confirm the flex."

Under the third framework, which our more philosophically inclined analysts have proposed and which the rest of the team has accepted under mild protest, all muscles flexed for social purposes are in some sense performed, and the line between "real" and "fake" in this context collapses under scrutiny. Every flex is a flex. The sincerity of the underlying musculature is secondary to the social function of the gesture. In this reading, Nayana Baiju flexed with intention and in context, and whether the muscles were "real" is a question that the moment itself renders somewhat beside the point.

What can be said with full confidence is this: the flex occurred. The briefing document is clear on this. The flex is documented. Its authenticity is disputed. Its existence is not. The Daily Forensic has filed the flex in the confirmed events column, placed the muscle assessment in the disputed findings column, and moved on with the rest of the dossier. Should further evidence emerge, we will update the record accordingly.
